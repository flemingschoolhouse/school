A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. An independent clause is a complete thought — it can stand alone as a sentence (The choir sang.). Week 2 · Atoms & the Periodic Table · Inside an Atom
Nevaeh

All matter is made of atoms — so small that millions could fit across the period at the end of this sentence. Each atom has a center called the nucleus, which holds protons (positive charge, +) and neutrons (no charge, 0). Whizzing around the nucleus in the electron cloud are electrons (negative charge, −), which are far lighter. In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons, so the charges balance.

History
Week 2 · The Thirteen Colonies · Jamestown, 1607
Nevaeh

In 1607, the Virginia Company of London sent about 100 men to build Jamestown — the first permanent English settlement in North America. They chose swampy land, many came hoping for gold rather than to farm, and disease and hunger killed most of them. Captain John Smith enforced a rule: “he that will not work shall not eat.” The winter of 1609–10, called the Starving Time, nearly wiped the colony out. Jamestown survived when John Rolfe began growing tobacco to sell in England. In 1619, Virginia formed the House of Burgesses, the first elected lawmaking body in the colonies — and that same year the first enslaved Africans were brought to Virginia, beginning a terrible injustice that would last for 250 years.

Kitchen Science Lab · Magic Milk
Nevaeh

Materials: a shallow plate, milk (whole works best), food coloring, dish soap, cotton swabs.

Why it works: milk is mostly water with tiny bits of fat and protein floating in it. The surface of the milk is pulled tight like a stretched skin — that's surface tension. Soap weakens that pull right where it touches, so the tighter surface around it yanks outward, dragging the colors. Soap molecules also grab onto fat, and as they chase the fat around, the swirling keeps going.

Week 2 · Atoms & the Periodic Table · Atomic Number & Mass
Nevaeh

Every element has its own atomic number = the number of protons. Change the protons and you change the element! The mass number = protons + neutrons. So: neutrons = mass number − atomic number. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons. Example: Nitrogen (N) has atomic number 7 and mass number 14 → 7 protons, 7 electrons, 14 − 7 = 7 neutrons.

Week 2 · The Thirteen Colonies · Plymouth & New England
Nevaeh

In 1620 the Pilgrims, who wanted to separate from the Church of England, sailed on the Mayflower. Before landing they signed the Mayflower Compact, agreeing to make fair laws and obey them — an early step toward self-government. New England's rocky soil and cold winters made farming hard, so people turned to fishing, shipbuilding, lumber, and trade, and governed through town meetings.

Nevaeh

A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause, joined by a subordinating conjunction (after, although, as, because, before, if, since, though, unless, until, when, while). Week 2 · Atoms & the Periodic Table · The Periodic Table
Nevaeh

The periodic table lists all known elements in order of atomic number. The rows are periods (7 of them); the columns are groups (18). Elements in the same group act alike because they have the same number of outer electrons. Metals (shiny, conduct electricity) are on the left and middle; nonmetals are on the right; metalloids sit along the zig-zag “staircase” between them. Group 1 metals react wildly with water; Group 18 noble gases (helium, neon, argon) barely react at all. Use any periodic table (online or in a book) for today's questions.

Nevaeh

The Middle Colonies — New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, and Delaware — had fertile soil and a milder climate. They grew so much wheat, corn, and rye that they were called the “breadbasket colonies.” New York began as the Dutch colony of New Netherland; the English took it in 1664. He made peace treaties with the Lenape people, and Philadelphia (“city of brotherly love”) grew into the largest city in the colonies. The Middle Colonies were the most diverse — Dutch, English, German, Swedish, Scots-Irish, and more lived side by side.

Week 2 · Atoms & the Periodic Table · Elements, Compounds & Mixtures
Nevaeh

An element is made of only one kind of atom (gold, iron, oxygen). A compound is two or more elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio — and it acts completely different from its parts. Sodium is an explosive metal and chlorine is a poisonous gas, but bonded together they make table salt! A mixture is substances physically mixed but not bonded, so they can be separated (sand and iron filings, salt water). A chemical formula shows the atoms in a compound: H₂O = 2 hydrogen + 1 oxygen.
