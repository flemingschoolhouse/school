Week 3 · Habitats & Adaptations · Habitats
Naomi

A habitat is the home where a plant or animal lives. It gives four things: food, water, shelter, and space. Forests have many trees (deer, owls, squirrels). Deserts get less than 10 inches of rain a year (camels, lizards, cactus). Oceans are salt water (sea turtles, dolphins, coral). Wetlands are land soaked with water, like marshes and swamps (herons, alligators). Grasslands are wide open fields of grass (bison, zebras, prairie dogs). Last week's food webs all live inside habitats!

Florida History
Week 3 · Spanish & French Florida · Ponce de León, 1513
Naomi

Juan Ponce de León sailed with Christopher Columbus on his second voyage in 1493 and later ruled Puerto Rico. In April 1513 he landed on Florida's east coast — no one knows the exact spot. It was the Easter season, called Pascua Florida (“flowery Easter”) in Spanish, and the land was full of flowers, so he named it La Florida. The “Fountain of Youth” story was mostly added later. In 1521 he came back to start a colony on the southwest coast, but the Calusa fought back and he was wounded by an arrow. He died soon after in Cuba.

Naomi

A linking verb doesn't show action. It links the subject to a word that names or describes it. The most common are forms of be: am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been. Week 3 · Habitats & Adaptations · Physical Adaptations
Naomi

An adaptation is a feature that helps a living thing survive in its habitat. Physical adaptations are body parts or body features: a duck's webbed feet paddle; a camel's hump stores fat and its long eyelashes block sand; a polar bear's thick fur and blubber keep it warm; a cactus's spines protect it and its waxy stem holds water. Camouflage is coloring that blends in (a flounder on sand). Mimicry means looking like something dangerous — Florida's harmless scarlet kingsnake looks like a venomous coral snake!

Week 3 · Spanish & French Florida · Fort Caroline — French in Jacksonville
Naomi

France wanted a piece of the New World too. In 1562 Jean Ribault sailed into the mouth of the St. Johns River, called it the River May, and set up a stone column to claim the land for France. In June 1564 René de Laudonnière brought about 200 settlers and built Fort Caroline, a triangle-shaped fort of earth and wood on the St. Johns — in today's Jacksonville! Today you can visit Fort Caroline National Memorial on the river (the fort there is a model — the real spot has never been found).

Naomi

Claude Monet (1840–1926) was a French painter and a leader of Impressionism — painting quick, loose brushstrokes to capture light and color in a moment instead of every tiny detail. DAY 3
Science
Week 3 · Habitats & Adaptations · Behavioral Adaptations
Naomi

Behavioral adaptations are things an animal does to survive. Migration: moving to a new place each season — monarch butterflies fly to Mexico, and American white pelicans spend the winter in Florida. Hibernation: a deep winter sleep where the body slows way down (groundhogs, some bats). Opossums play dead; fish swim in schools; many animals hunt at night (nocturnal). Camouflage works best with a behavior: a rabbit freezes so its brown fur hides it. Many behaviors are instincts — God built them in, no one teaches them.

DAY 3
Florida History
Week 3 · Spanish & French Florida · Menéndez & St. Augustine
Naomi

King Philip II of Spain was angry that France was building in Florida, so he sent Pedro Menéndez de Avilés. Menéndez sighted land on August 28, 1565 — the feast day of St. Augustine, so that became the town's name. On September 8, 1565, he came ashore near a Timucua village, held a church service, and shared a meal — 56 years before the Pilgrims' Thanksgiving. The French under Ribault sailed south to attack, but a hurricane wrecked their ships. Menéndez marched his soldiers about 40 miles north through the storm and captured Fort Caroline on September 20, 1565, renaming it San Mateo. Many French were killed, and Spain took control of Florida.

Naomi

A verb's tense tells when. Past = already happened: most verbs add -ed (walked); if it ends in consonant + y, change y to i (hurried). Irregular verbs change their spelling (run → ran, eat → ate, go → went). Present = now (walks, walk). Future = will happen: use the helping verb will (will walk). Keep one tense in a story unless the time changes.

Naomi

Florida is full of habitats! The St. Johns River is Florida's longest river (about 310 miles) and one of the few that flows north — right through Jacksonville to the Atlantic. Its wetlands clean water and are nurseries for fish. The manatee is a gentle herbivore that eats seagrass; cold water below about 68°F can make it sick, so in winter it migrates to warm springs like Blue Spring on the St. Johns. The alligator has eyes and nostrils on top of its head so it can hide underwater and still see and breathe. The gopher tortoise lives in dry, sandy uplands and uses shovel-like front legs to dig burrows about 15 feet long — more than 350 other kinds of animals use them!

DAY 4
Florida History
Week 3 · Spanish & French Florida · Missions & the Castillo
Naomi

In the 1600s, Spanish Franciscan friars built missions across north Florida to teach the Timucua, Guale, and Apalachee people about Christianity (you can visit rebuilt Mission San Luis in Tallahassee). St. Augustine had only wooden forts, and in 1668 the English pirate Robert Searles raided the town. So Spain built a stone fort: the Castillo de San Marcos, from 1672 to 1695. It is made of coquina — a rock of tiny shells stuck together — dug on Anastasia Island. Cannonballs sank into the soft stone instead of cracking it! In 1702 English soldiers from Carolina burned the town, but about 1,500 people sheltered inside for nearly two months and the Castillo never fell. It is the oldest masonry (stone) fort in the continental U.S.
