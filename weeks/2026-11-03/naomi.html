Week 2 · Food Chains & Food Webs · Producers & Consumers
Naomi

Every living thing needs energy. Producers make their own food from sunlight — plants, algae, and seagrass. Consumers eat other living things. Herbivores eat only plants (manatee, deer). Carnivores eat only animals (alligator, osprey). Omnivores eat both (raccoon, bear, people!). God designed it so that all the energy in a food chain starts with the sun.

Week 2 · Florida's First Peoples · The First Floridians
Naomi

People have lived in Florida for a very long time — archaeologists believe more than 12,000 years. Back then Florida was cooler and drier, the coastline stretched much farther out, and giant animals like mammoths and mastodons roamed. The first Floridians hunted, fished, and gathered plants. Over thousands of years they built villages, made pottery, and piled up huge mounds of shells called middens — some are still standing today! By the time Europeans arrived, about 350,000 Native people lived in Florida.

English
Week 2 · All About Nouns · Plural Nouns
Naomi

Plural means more than one. Most nouns add -s (cats). Nouns ending in s, x, z, ch, sh add -es (boxes, dishes). Consonant + y: change y to i and add -es (baby → babies), but vowel + y just adds -s (toy → toys). Some f/fe words change to -ves (leaf → leaves). And some are irregular — they change completely or not at all: child → children, mouse → mice, tooth → teeth, sheep → sheep.

Florida History
Week 2 · Florida's First Peoples · The Timucua — Our Neighbors!
Naomi

The Timucua (tee-MOO-kwah) lived in northeast and north-central Florida — right where Jacksonville is today! They lived in villages of round houses made of poles and palm thatch, with a large council house in the center. They farmed corn, beans, and squash, and also fished, hunted deer, and gathered oysters along the St. Johns River in dugout canoes. Timucua leaders often had elaborate tattoos to show their rank. Sadly, after Europeans arrived, war and new diseases caused the Timucua population to shrink until they disappeared by the 1700s.

Artist Study · Henri Matisse · Painting with Scissors
Naomi

Henri Matisse (1869–1954) was a French artist famous for bold, bright colors. Late in life, after a serious illness, he often couldn't stand at an easel — so he invented a new way to make art. Assistants painted big sheets of paper, and Matisse “drew with scissors,” cutting out leaves, stars, swirls and wavy shapes, then arranging them on the wall. He called them cut-outs. Week 2 · Food Chains & Food Webs · Decomposers
Naomi

When plants and animals die, who cleans up? Decomposers! Bacteria, fungi (mushrooms and mold), and helpers like earthworms break down dead things and waste and return nutrients to the soil. Then producers use those nutrients to grow, and the cycle starts again. Without decomposers, the earth would be piled with dead leaves and the soil would run out of nutrients. Try it: look under a log or in the leaf pile in the backyard!

Florida History
Week 2 · Florida's First Peoples · Europeans Arrive
Naomi

In 1513, Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León landed on the east coast and named the land La Florida — “land of flowers.” In 1564, French Protestants (Huguenots) built Fort Caroline on the St. Johns River — in what is now Jacksonville! Spain wanted Florida for itself, so in 1565 Pedro Menéndez de Avilés founded St. Augustine and soon destroyed Fort Caroline. St. Augustine is the oldest continuously inhabited city founded by Europeans in the United States — older than Jamestown by 42 years!

Week 2 · Latitude & Longitude
Naomi

Latitude lines run east–west (like rungs on a ladder) and measure how far north or south of the equator a place is (0° to 90°). Longitude lines run north–south, pole to pole, and measure how far east or west of the prime meridian a place is (0° to 180°). Write latitude first: Jacksonville is about 30°N, 82°W.
